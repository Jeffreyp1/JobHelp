# Priority Hierarchy

When two rules conflict, resolve in this fixed order. Higher items always win.

## 1. Accuracy

The candidate's actual record. Dates, titles, employers, scope, ownership, publication status. Never trade an accurate weaker claim for an inaccurate stronger one. When in doubt, hedge.

## 2. Relevance

What the JD actually asks for. A truthful but irrelevant achievement is worse than a truthful relevant one. Allocate bullets and skills space by JD relevance, not by personal pride.

## 3. Impact

Quantified outcomes the reader can grade. Numbers, names, scale, adoption, verified results. An impactful bullet beats a vague one when both are accurate and relevant.

## 4. ATS

Verbatim keyword presence so the document survives automated screening. Use the JD's exact terms when they fit the candidate's truthful work. Never stuff keywords that would force inaccuracy.

## 5. Brevity

Char limits, line counts, page budgets. Always last. Cut padding before cutting content. If trimming for brevity would force inaccuracy, push back on the budget instead.

## Conflict examples

- Recruiter wants more keywords (ATS) but the only matching bullet is overclaim (Accuracy): keep the truthful version, lose the keyword.
- A 2-line bullet is over the char limit (Brevity) but every word adds JD-specific impact (Impact + ATS): trim wording, never invent missing details.
- A more impressive metric exists in the experience file but belongs to a collaborator (Accuracy beats Impact): use the user's smaller verified number.
- A page is over budget (Brevity) and one bullet is off-topic (Relevance): cut the off-topic bullet first.
